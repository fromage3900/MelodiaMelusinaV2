# ANMN 304 - Production Bible Outline (due 10/9, 2 attempts possible)

## What the Production Bible is

From the 304 outline: The Production Bible is a planning document for the group animated short film. It is 30% of the course grade. The outline says students will "compose storyboards and concept art as planning tools for the project" and "develop a pipeline that meets the requirements of the project." The Production Bible is where all of that lives.

The outline assessment weighting for 304:
- Production Bible: 30%
- Production Meetings: 30%
- Final Project: 40%
- Project post-mortem: S/U

---

## Section-by-section outline

### 1. Title Page
- Film title
- Team member names and roles
- Course code (ANMN 304), semester (Fall 2026)
- Date

### 2. Logline and Story Summary
- One-sentence logline
- 1-2 paragraph story summary
- Tone and genre
- Target audience

**What you may already have:** The pitch session was 9/18 and received 80/100. That pitch likely contained a logline and story concept. Check your pitch materials.

### 3. Storyboards
- Panel-by-panel breakdown of the film
- Shot type, duration, action description (see the Shorewake beat board in `ANMN301_Animatic/SHOREWAKE_BEAT_BOARD.md` for the format)
- Numbered panels with on-panel labels

**What you may already have:** If your group has started storyboards, include them. If not, this is a gap to fill before 10/9.

### 4. Concept Art and Art Direction
- Character designs (turnarounds, expression sheets)
- Environment/prop designs
- Color palette and mood boards
- Lighting style references

**What you may already have:** In Class 1 (9/25) required "images of the week's work: rigs found, designs, concepts." If you submitted designs for In Class 1, those can be refined and included here.

### 5. Pipeline and Workflow
- Tools and software (Blender, Unreal, etc.)
- Asset management (naming conventions, folder structure)
- Workflow per stage (modeling, rigging, animation, lighting, rendering, compositing)
- File formats and delivery specs

**What you may already have:** If your group has discussed or agreed on a pipeline, document it here. If not, this is a gap.

### 6. Production Schedule
- Week-by-week breakdown of tasks
- Milestones and deadlines
- Dependencies (what must be done before something else can start)
- Contingency time

**What you may already have:** The 304 outline mentions "adhere to a well-planned production schedule." If your group has a schedule, include it. If not, create one.

### 7. Team Roles and Responsibilities
- Who does what (modeling, rigging, animation, lighting, compositing, sound, editing)
- Contact information
- Availability and time commitments

**What you may already have:** The Group Role List exists in the Blackboard course content. Check it and include it.

### 8. References and Inspiration
- Films, games, art that inspired the project
- Why they are relevant
- How they inform the art direction or story
