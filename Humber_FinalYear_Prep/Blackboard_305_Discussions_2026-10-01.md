## Week 3 — "CENSORSHIP" (prompt)

In light of our class that included a talk about the Hays Commission and the Production Code,
let's talk about censorship. Censorship has always been – and I suspect always will be – a
sensitive topic. There are deeply held, personal and often conflicting beliefs. It can be a
deeply problematic one for artists. While I work to keep my own opinions out of the mix (too
much) I will say on this topic, that I suggest that you avoid extreme, all inclusive
statements. Statements like "it's wrong, we should have no censorship at all" opens the door
to abuses like child pornography, yet our history books are full of rulers and governments who
tried to control freedom of speech and self-expression; those measures always go hand in hand
with rather ruthless dictators. I'm a believer in the middle ground…not that you have to be.

Some things to consider for discussion (in no particular order):
These are difficult subjects and my intention is not to start an argument, but to make you
think about, examine and clarify your own thoughts on this very challenging topic.
As always, I request that you be honest but respectful. We don't need to – and probably won't
– agree, but we do need to be respectful of differences…or am I just trying to censor you?

(Thread: rating systems ESRB/RIAA/MPA; Steven Universe censorship abroad; hate speech; Betty
Boop; social-media age detection; "censorship should protect, not manipulate".)

## Week 4 — "Plan B...or C...or" (prompt)

Consider Walt Disney in the days before Steamboat Willie became the success that has made the
studio famous. He's declared bankruptcy. He's had his great character Oswald taken from him,
along with all but one of his animators. He's had two cartoons with his new character (Micky
Mouse) that have not seen a single audience. To many people he should give up...but he didn't.
This story, and many like it, have inspired people for ages, what we don't hear about are the
thousands who didn't give up, but never made it, or about the hundreds who learned from their
failures and took another path that led to success. I'm an example of this, when I was in my
early 20's all I wanted to do was perform, now, I LOVE my life as a teacher and a writer. Many
would say I gave up.

I suspect that this topic will have a lot less back and forth discussion, (and that's OK -
I'll take that into consideration when assessing marks) as it's a deeply personal thing. For
this week I'm hoping to make you think about plan B...or do you have one?
I'm NOT trying to discourage anyone from your dreams - - - I'm a HUGE believer in following
your dreams, but I've also discovered that there are times in life when your dreams change.

Some things to consider:
- What's the difference between changing your dream and giving up?
- Is there a difference?
- Do you have a "plan B...or C"? Should you?
- At the same time, I would encourage you to think about what success actually means...TO YOU!
  What's the "win" you're hoping for from animation, your career or life?
