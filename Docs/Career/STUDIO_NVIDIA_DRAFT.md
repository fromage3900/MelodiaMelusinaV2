## How to actually get in — honest process notes

- **68% rejection rate at interview stage.** Most candidates underestimate
  domain specificity. Generic enthusiasm fails.
- **Decentralized hiring.** No central committee — the hiring manager on the
  team you'd join makes the call. Research that specific team's output before
  the screen.
- **Referrals matter, but must be earned.** Do not cold-DM engineers asking
  for referrals. Engage with their published work, open-source PRs, or conference
  talks first. A credible L4/L5 referral is worth ~10x a cold application.
- **Vector Institute is the back channel.** NV is a platinum sponsor through
  2027. Attending Vector events or engaging with affiliated researchers
  (Animesh Garg, Fidler lab alumni) is the highest-leverage networking path in
  Toronto specifically.
- **"Why NVIDIA" must be team-specific.** "I love GPUs" fails. For Toronto/SIL:
  cite specific Fidler lab papers (neural scene representations, 3D-GS, annotation
  tooling). For DevRel: cite the Vector partnership and the gap between academic
  research and production deployment.
- **Interview loop:** recruiter screen → OA (HackerRank) → 1-2 technical phone
  screens → 4-6 round virtual loop (coding, system design, domain deep dive,
  behavioral) → team match → calibration → offer. Timeline: 4–8 weeks, referrals
  faster.
- **C++ is expected** for engineering tracks even if the day-to-day is Python.
  For DevRel, it's less critical but systems literacy is tested.
