> **Purpose:** build a legally sane reference crate for studying hyperpop / PC Music production language and sourcing commercially usable sound-design material for Melodia.
>
> **Hard rule:** an artist releasing stems for remixing or study does **not** automatically grant permission to ship those stems, derivatives, isolated vocals, or recognizable musical material inside a game. Treat artist stems as **RESEARCH / REMIX / PRIVATE PROTOTYPE** unless the accompanying license explicitly grants commercial reuse. For shipped Melodia audio, prefer clearly royalty-free or directly licensed sources and retain proof of license.

**Official source:**  
https://soundcloud.com/pcmus/superstar

The original PC Music SoundCloud post explicitly advertises **“Download stems”** and points to the historical `SUPERSTEMS` link.

### What to study

- vocal stack width and placement;
- supersaw / synthetic-pad density without losing the lead;
- hard brightness against soft sentimental harmony;
- macro-dynamic contrast;
- how a very simple emotional idea survives extreme synthetic production.

### Melodia translation

Use as reference for **Convergence / Resonance states that should feel emotionally sincere rather than cyberpunk**. The useful lesson is not “make it loud”; it is **make the artificial texture carry a vulnerable melody**.

**License bucket:** `RESEARCH_ONLY_PENDING_LICENSE`.

# ♬ Tier C — commercially useful, clearly royalty-free sound-design lane

## Splice licensing guardrail

Current Splice hyperpop pages state that samples are **100% royalty-free** and cleared for commercial use, subject to Splice's Terms of Use. Splice also advertises downloadable license documentation for proof of licensed use.

**General reference:**  
https://splice.com/sounds/genres/hyperpop/fx/samples

For anything that might ship in Melodia:

1. download through the licensed account;
2. retain the pack/sample filename;
3. retain Splice license documentation / receipt if available;
4. transform and compose rather than dumping raw packs into Content;
5. log source in `Docs/SOURCES_MATRIX.md` or the eventual audio provenance manifest.

# ♬ Musical design lessons to steal without stealing audio

Across these sources, the recurring high-value ideas are:

1. **Materialized percussion** — a hit can sound like an object, substance or impossible physical event.
2. **Earnest melody under synthetic violence** — emotional clarity can survive extreme timbre.
3. **Density is automated** — maximalism works because it is contrasted with sudden emptiness.
4. **Genre is vocabulary, not allegiance** — trance, pop, rave, noise, acoustic fragments and jokes can coexist if hierarchy is clear.
5. **Vocals are geometry** — pitch, formant, chop length, stereo placement and reverb define shape as much as lyrics do.
6. **Transitions deserve authored assets** — fills, reverses, fake-outs and micro-stingers are structural tools, not leftovers.
7. **Tiny sounds can carry identity** — UI, wardrobe, footsteps and interactions can share the same sonic material language as battle music.
8. **Supplementary files can be worldbuilding** — MIDI, patches, images and 3D artifacts can make music feel like part of a larger authored place.

For Melodia, the target is not “sound like PC Music.” It is:

> **Make musical production behave like another layer of world material — cloth, water, glass, metal, memory and rhythm all able to become sound.**
